When I design with AI, I can prompt something wild in a few minutes. A landing page or a full dashboard. It looks good, and it's fast.

The problem starts when this becomes the standard. If everyone can build a Ferrari, a Ferrari is no longer special. The bar goes up again, and we're back at the old question: what makes this one better?

## Taste is still the hard part

The answer is still taste. Knowing what to leave out, and when something is ready.

That taste sits in a designer's head. The machine doesn't have it. It has seen a lot, and by default it gives you the average of it. So the work moves, from making the thing to getting the taste from my head into the machine.

## How the machine reads English

To do that, I need to learn how the machine reads my words. How it turns plain English into choices about type and spacing. A sentence that is clear to me can mean something quite different to the model.

That's a new kind of craft, somewhere between design and engineering. I don't think we have a good name for it yet.

## Words that match the picture in your head

The person who learns to feed the machine the words that match their imagination wins the trophy. Not the one with the fastest prompt.

For me, that's skill engineering. I write my taste down as rules and skills the AI can follow, test them on real work, and refine them like any other code. I'm still learning how much of taste fits into words.
